    Motion in a Straight Line

    5 minute read. Tap any underlined term for a quick recap.

    Key definitions

    Displacement is the shortest vector from the initial to the final position. Velocity is the rate of change of displacement, and acceleration is the rate of change of velocity.

    Equations of uniformly accelerated motion

    v = u + at
s = ut + ½at²
v² = u² + 2as

    Points to remember

    • The slope of an x–t graph gives velocity.
    • The slope of a v–t graph gives acceleration.
    • The area under a v–t graph gives displacement.
    • For a projectile, the range is greatest at 45° to the horizon.

    Practice question

    A ball is thrown upward at 20 m/s. Find the maximum height reached (g = 10 m/s²).

    Show answerH = u²/2g = 400/20 = 20 m

    Laws of Motion

    6 minute read.

    Newton's laws

    The first law introduces inertia: a body stays at rest or in uniform motion unless a net force acts on it. The second law links force to the rate of change of momentum, F = dp/dt. The third law pairs every action with an equal and opposite reaction.

    Friction

    Static friction adjusts itself up to a limiting value μₛN. Once sliding starts, kinetic friction μₖN acts, and it is usually smaller.

    Equilibrium

    A body is in equilibrium when the vector sum of all forces on it is zero. Draw the free-body diagram, then resolve the forces along two perpendicular axes.

    Practice question

    A 2 kg block rests on a rough surface with μₛ = 0.5. What is the maximum horizontal force before it slides? (g = 10 m/s²)

    Show answerF = μₛmg = 0.5 × 2 × 10 = 10 N

    Work, Energy & Gravitation

    7 minute read.

    Work–energy theorem

    The net work done on a body equals the change in its kinetic energy. When only conservative forces act, total mechanical energy stays constant.

    Simple pendulum

    A pendulum converts potential energy into kinetic energy and back. For small angles the time period is T = 2π√(L/g), independent of mass. When the driving frequency matches the natural frequency, resonance occurs and the amplitude grows.

    Gravitation

    Newton's law of gravity states F = Gm₁m₂/r². A satellite in a circular orbit moves at v = √(GM/r), and the escape velocity from Earth is about 11.2 km/s.

    Practice question

    Find the time period of a pendulum of length 1 m (g = π² m/s²).

    Show answerT = 2π√(1/π²) = 2 s

    Some Basic Concepts of Chemistry

    6 minute read.

    The mole

    One mole contains 6.022 × 10²³ particles. The molar mass in grams per mole equals the atomic or molecular mass in u. The number of moles is mass divided by molar mass.

    Concentration terms

    Molarity   M = moles of solute / litres of solution
Molality   m = moles of solute / kg of solvent
Mole fraction  x = moles of component / total moles

    Limiting reagent

    The reactant that is used up first decides how much product forms. Convert every reactant to moles, divide by its coefficient, and the smallest value is the limiting reagent.

    Practice question

    How many moles are in 36 g of water (H₂O = 18 g/mol)?

    Show answern = 36 / 18 = 2 mol

    Structure of Atom

    7 minute read.

    Quantum numbers

    • Principal (n) gives the shell and its size.
    • Azimuthal (l) gives the subshell shape, from 0 to n − 1.
    • Magnetic (mₗ) gives the orbital orientation, from −l to +l.
    • Spin (mₛ) is +½ or −½.

    Filling rules

    Electrons fill the lowest energy orbital first (Aufbau), no two electrons share all four quantum numbers (Pauli), and orbitals of equal energy fill singly before pairing (Hund).

    Bohr radius and energy

    rₙ = 52.9 pm × n² / Z
Eₙ = −13.6 eV × Z² / n²

    Practice question

    What is the maximum number of electrons in the n = 3 shell?

    Show answer2n² = 2 × 9 = 18

    Sets and Relations

    5 minute read.

    Set operations

    The union A ∪ B holds elements in either set, and the intersection A ∩ B holds elements in both. The complement A′ holds everything in the universal set that is not in A.

    Useful results

    n(A ∪ B) = n(A) + n(B) − n(A ∩ B)
n(P(A)) = 2ⁿ   for a set with n elements
(A ∪ B)′ = A′ ∩ B′

    Relations

    A relation from A to B is any subset of A × B. It is a function when every element of A has exactly one image in B.

    Practice question

    If n(A) = 20, n(B) = 15 and n(A ∩ B) = 8, find n(A ∪ B).

    Show answer20 + 15 − 8 = 27

    Trigonometric Functions

    6 minute read.

    Core identities

    sin²x + cos²x = 1
1 + tan²x = sec²x
sin(A + B) = sinA cosB + cosA sinB
cos(A + B) = cosA cosB − sinA sinB

    Quick values

    sin 30° = ½, sin 45° = 1/√2, sin 60° = √3/2. The cosine values run in the reverse order.

    Radian measure

    π radians equal 180°. An arc of length l on a circle of radius r subtends an angle l/r radians.

    Practice question

    Find sin 75° using the sum formula.

    Show answersin(45° + 30°) = (1/√2)(√3/2) + (1/√2)(½) = (√3 + 1)/(2√2)
