    Motion in a Straight Line

    5 minute read. Tap any underlined term for a quick recap.

    Key definitions

    Displacement is the shortest vector from the initial to the final position. Velocity is the rate of change of displacement, and acceleration is the rate of change of velocity.

    Equations of uniformly accelerated motion

    v = u + at
s = ut + ½at²
v² = u² + 2as

    Points to remember

    • The slope of an x–t graph gives velocity.
    • The slope of a v–t graph gives acceleration.
    • The area under a v–t graph gives displacement.
    • For a projectile, the range is greatest at 45° to the horizon.

    Practice question

    A ball is thrown upward at 20 m/s. Find the maximum height reached (g = 10 m/s²).

    Show answerH = u²/2g = 400/20 = 20 m

    Laws of Motion

    6 minute read.

    Newton's laws

    The first law introduces inertia: a body stays at rest or in uniform motion unless a net force acts on it. The second law links force to the rate of change of momentum, F = dp/dt. The third law pairs every action with an equal and opposite reaction.

    Friction

    Static friction adjusts itself up to a limiting value μₛN. Once sliding starts, kinetic friction μₖN acts, and it is usually smaller.

    Equilibrium

    A body is in equilibrium when the vector sum of all forces on it is zero. Draw the free-body diagram, then resolve the forces along two perpendicular axes.

    Practice question

    A 2 kg block rests on a rough surface with μₛ = 0.5. What is the maximum horizontal force before it slides? (g = 10 m/s²)

    Show answerF = μₛmg = 0.5 × 2 × 10 = 10 N

    Work, Energy and Gravitation

    7 minute read.

    Work–energy theorem

    The net work done on a body equals the change in its kinetic energy. When only conservative forces act, total mechanical energy stays constant.

    Simple pendulum

    A pendulum converts potential energy into kinetic energy and back. For small angles the time period is T = 2π√(L/g), independent of mass. When the driving frequency matches the natural frequency, resonance occurs and the amplitude grows.

    Gravitation

    Newton's law of gravity states F = Gm₁m₂/r². A satellite in a circular orbit moves at v = √(GM/r), and the escape velocity from Earth is about 11.2 km/s.

    Practice question

    Find the time period of a pendulum of length 1 m (g = π² m/s²).

    Show answerT = 2π√(1/π²) = 2 s

    Current Electricity

    6 minute read.

    Ohm's law and resistivity

    V = IR
R = ρL / A
P = VI = I²R = V²/R

    Resistance rises with length and falls with cross-sectional area. For metals, resistivity increases with temperature.

    Combinations of resistors

    Series:    R = R₁ + R₂ + …        (same current)
Parallel:  1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + …  (same voltage)

    Kirchhoff's rules

    • Junction rule: the sum of currents entering a junction equals the sum leaving it (charge conservation).
    • Loop rule: the algebraic sum of potential changes around a closed loop is zero (energy conservation).

    Practice question

    Resistors of 6 Ω and 3 Ω are connected in parallel across a 6 V battery. Find the total current drawn.

    Show answerR = (6 × 3)/(6 + 3) = 2 Ω, so I = 6/2 = 3 A

    Some Basic Concepts of Chemistry

    6 minute read.

    The mole

    One mole contains 6.022 × 10²³ particles. The molar mass in grams per mole equals the atomic or molecular mass in u. The number of moles is mass divided by molar mass.

    Concentration terms

    Molarity       M = moles of solute / litres of solution
Molality       m = moles of solute / kg of solvent
Mole fraction  x = moles of component / total moles

    Limiting reagent

    The reactant that is used up first decides how much product forms. Convert every reactant to moles, divide by its coefficient, and the smallest value is the limiting reagent.

    Practice question

    How many moles are in 36 g of water (H₂O = 18 g/mol)?

    Show answern = 36 / 18 = 2 mol

    Structure of Atom

    7 minute read.

    Quantum numbers

    • Principal (n) gives the shell and its size.
    • Azimuthal (l) gives the subshell shape, from 0 to n − 1.
    • Magnetic (mₗ) gives the orbital orientation, from −l to +l.
    • Spin (mₛ) is +½ or −½.

    Filling rules

    Electrons fill the lowest energy orbital first (Aufbau), no two electrons share all four quantum numbers (Pauli), and orbitals of equal energy fill singly before pairing (Hund).

    Bohr radius and energy

    rₙ = 52.9 pm × n² / Z
Eₙ = −13.6 eV × Z² / n²

    Practice question

    What is the maximum number of electrons in the n = 3 shell?

    Show answer2n² = 2 × 9 = 18

    Chemical Bonding

    7 minute read.

    Types of bonds

    An ionic bond forms by transfer of electrons, a covalent bond by sharing of electrons, and a coordinate bond when one atom donates both electrons of the shared pair.

    Hybridisation and shape

    sp   linear            180°      e.g. BeCl₂, CO₂
sp²  trigonal planar   120°      e.g. BF₃
sp³  tetrahedral       109.5°    e.g. CH₄

    Lone pairs squeeze bond angles, so NH₃ (about 107°) and H₂O (about 104.5°) are smaller than 109.5°.

    Bond order (molecular orbital theory)

    Bond order = (Nb − Na) / 2

    A higher bond order means a shorter, stronger bond. A bond order of zero means the molecule does not exist.

    Practice question

    N₂ has 10 electrons in bonding orbitals and 4 in antibonding orbitals. Find its bond order.

    Show answer(10 − 4) / 2 = 3

    Cell: The Unit of Life

    7 minute read.

    Cell theory

    Schleiden and Schwann proposed that all organisms are made of cells. Virchow added that new cells arise only from pre-existing cells (omnis cellula e cellula).

    Prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells

    • Prokaryotes have no membrane-bound nucleus or organelles, and their ribosomes are 70S.
    • Eukaryotes have a true nucleus and organelles, and their cytoplasmic ribosomes are 80S.
    • The cell wall of bacteria contains peptidoglycan, that of plants contains cellulose, and that of fungi contains chitin.

    Key organelles

    Mitochondria have a double membrane, their own circular DNA and 70S ribosomes, and they are the site of aerobic respiration. The Golgi apparatus packages and modifies proteins, and lysosomes hold hydrolytic enzymes.

    Practice question

    Which type of ribosomes is found inside mitochondria?

    Show answer70S ribosomes, similar to those of prokaryotes.

    Photosynthesis in Higher Plants

    8 minute read.

    Where it happens

    The light reactions occur in the thylakoid membranes of the grana, where chlorophyll a is the main pigment. The Calvin cycle occurs in the stroma.

    Calvin cycle (C3 pathway)

    The enzyme RuBisCO fixes CO₂ onto RuBP, a 5-carbon sugar, to form two molecules of 3-phosphoglyceric acid (PGA). PGA is reduced using ATP and NADPH, and RuBP is regenerated.

    For one glucose (6 CO₂ fixed):
18 ATP and 12 NADPH are used

    C4 plants

    • The first stable product is oxaloacetic acid (a 4-carbon compound), made by PEP carboxylase in mesophyll cells.
    • Kranz anatomy places the Calvin cycle in the bundle sheath cells.
    • Photorespiration is negligible, so C4 plants are efficient at high temperature and light.

    Practice question

    How many ATP molecules does the Calvin cycle use to make one glucose molecule?

    Show answer18 ATP (3 ATP per CO₂ fixed, for 6 CO₂).

    Principles of Inheritance and Variation

    8 minute read.

    Mendel's laws

    • Dominance: in a heterozygote, one allele (the dominant one) masks the other.
    • Segregation: the two alleles of a gene separate during gamete formation.
    • Independent assortment: genes on different chromosomes assort independently.

    Standard ratios

    Monohybrid cross   phenotype 3 : 1,  genotype 1 : 2 : 1
Dihybrid cross     phenotype 9 : 3 : 3 : 1
Test cross (Aa × aa)           1 : 1
Dihybrid test cross            1 : 1 : 1 : 1

    The genotype is the genetic makeup, while the phenotype is the observable trait. Incomplete dominance gives a 1 : 2 : 1 phenotypic ratio, and codominance is seen in the AB blood group.

    Practice question

    In a cross Tt × Tt, what is the probability of a homozygous recessive offspring?

    Show answerTT : Tt : tt = 1 : 2 : 1, so tt = 1/4.
